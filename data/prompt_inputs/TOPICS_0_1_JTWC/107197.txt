Source Text, 107197 (Latin):
Intestati datur bonorum possessio per septem gradus: primo gradu 
liberis; secundo legitimis heredibus; tertio proximis cognatis; quarto 
familiae patroni; quinto patrono, patronae, item liberis parentibusve 
patroni patronaeve; sexto viro, uxori; septimo cognatis manumissoris, 
quibus per legem Furiam plus mile mille +  asses capere licet: et si nemo 
sit, ad quem bonorum possessio pertinere possit, aut sit quidem, sed ius 
suum omiserit, populo bona deferuntur ex lege Iulia caducaria.

English Translation:
The possession of an estate on the ground of intestacy is granted through seven degrees; in the first degree to children, in the second, to heirs-at-law; in the third to the nearest cognates; in the fourth to the family of the patron; in the fifth to the patron, the patroness, and their children, or their ascendants; in the sixth to the husband or wife; in the seventh to the cognates of the manumitted who are entitled under the Lex Furia to receive more than a thousand asses. If there is no one entitled to the possession of the estate, or if anyone fails to exercise his right, the estate will pass to the people under the Lex Julia having reference to inheritances without ownership.
